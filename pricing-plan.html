Duo Price Book

We sell the same AiR engagement at six different prices between $550 and $2,200 a month. This is the proposal to replace that with one published price book, a hard margin floor, and a rule for who may discount what.

Prepared by Kaden Myers, Operations Decision owner Lance Date 9 September 2026

What we need decided

Five decisions. Everything else in this document is evidence for them or mechanics that follow from them.

  1. Adopt three published AiR tiers at $1,200 / $1,500 / $2,500 per location.LanceReplaces the current practice of pricing each deal from scratch.
  2. Adopt a hard gross-margin floor of 50%, with list prices set to clear 60%.LanceUses the benchmark already in our own team-margin model. Below-floor deals need written sign-off.
  3. Stop bundling ad spend into the monthly fee.Lance · KadenRestore the 2024 campaign equation: management fee quoted separately from media, always.
  4. Tie included build work to a minimum term.Lance · MaxNo free website on a 7-day-out agreement. 12 months where a site is included.
  5. Retire the four undefined discount names and publish a discount authority matrix.LanceTwo programs survive: multi-location and annual prepay.

Read the two ends of that table together. Lawn Doctor pays $550 for a dashboard and a dedicated rep. That is a 4x price spread — but nothing like 4x the work separates the cheapest engagement from the most expensive.

The pattern runs the wrong way. The lower the price, the more we tend to promise relative to what the price can fund, because discounting happens on the number while the scope stays whole.

02

Why $650 cannot work

Every AiR dashboard costs us $225 a month before anyone touches the account. At the bottom of our price range that platform fee alone consumes 41% of revenue.

Our team-margin model sets a mid-level Digital Strategist at $5,500 a month carrying 8 accounts, and marks 60% gross margin as the threshold at which we can afford to hire. Those are our numbers, not benchmarks borrowed from outside. Put the $225 platform cost against them and the arithmetic is unforgiving.

The finding

At $550 a month there is no caseload that reaches 60% gross margin — the platform fee exceeds the entire 40% cost allowance before a single hour of work. At $650 a rep would need 158 dashboards. Our agreements at these prices promise a dedicated representative who “actively monitors your dashboard, identifies opportunities, tracks progress, and provides clear next steps.” One monthly call spends more than the price supports.

03

Three problems the price book has to fix

Discount names nobody defined

Four different labels appear in 2026 agreements: “partner discount,” “Neighbor partner rate,” “active client” pricing, and multi-location pricing. None is defined anywhere, none has a size, and none has an approval step. In practice a discount name is written after the number has already been agreed, to explain it.

Build work given away on a seven-day contract

Two agreements a week apart handled the same risk in opposite ways. The CMP proposal includes a full website and sets a six-month initial term, explicitly because signal work compounds. The Mill Forest agreement includes a full website with hosting and is month-to-month, terminable on seven days' notice.

A website is a $4,000 build on our own rate card. Under Mill Forest's terms a client can take delivery of that asset and leave in month two having paid $4,400. We have no clause that recovers it.

Live deal · act before signature

The CMP proposal's single-office tier prices AiR plus a full website at $1,500 a month against a six-month term. Amortising the build across that term puts the engagement at roughly 36% gross margin — below the 45% line our own model marks as “cannot hire.” The two- and three-office tiers carry the build across more revenue and hold up. The single-office tier should be repriced or the website removed from it before this goes out.

Media buying folded into the fee

Our proposal template reads “$____ per month, inclusive of any ad spend and full management.” Santiam Hospital is contracted at $22,698 a month with roughly $4,220 of monthly media — Google, LinkedIn and Meta — paid out of that figure. Lawn Doctor's campaigns are quoted “starting at $1,000/month (includes ad spend).”

Under that structure every dollar the client asks us to add to a campaign comes straight out of our margin, and there is no point at which we can say a budget increase costs more to manage. We already solved this. The 2024 rate card carries a campaign equation that separates the two and nobody uses it:

M = $1,500 + $250 × ( (T − $3,000) ÷ $1,000 ) A = T − M

M = account optimisation fee, A = ad spend, T = total paid by client. From the June 2024 Duo Pricing Sheet.

It is a sound structure — a declining percentage fee that never reaches zero: 50% of a $3,000 total, 40% at $5,000, 32.5% at $10,000. The recommendation is not to invent something. It is to publish this, quote fee and media as two lines on every agreement, and stop absorbing budget increases.

The floors are derived, not chosen: each is the price at which that tier's platform cost plus its delivery cap still leaves 50% gross margin. List prices sit above them so that a normal deal clears 60%.

Two tiers are close to what the market has already accepted. Kobico signed Signal scope at $1,500 and Mill Forest signed Complete scope at $2,200. Core at $1,200 matches what CIS accepted. The change is not a price rise across the board — it is the removal of the sub-$1,000 band, which we cannot deliver profitably at any volume.

Discounts

  • Multi-location. 15% off the second location, 25% off the third and beyond. Never below the tier floor. The $225 platform cost repeats per dashboard, so this discount comes out of account-management efficiency only.
  • Annual prepay. 8% off, paid in advance for twelve months.
  • These two stack to a maximum of 30%. “Partner,” “Neighbor” and “active client” rates are retired.

05

Rollout

Ninety days, structured so that nothing new goes out below floor from week one, and existing clients are handled last and slowly.

Days 1–15
Approve and freeze
  • Lance signs off the tiers, floors and discount matrix
  • No new agreement goes out below floor without written approval, effective immediately
  • Reprice or rescope the CMP single-office tier before it is sent
Days 16–30
Build the instruments
  • Rebuild the proposal template around a tier selection rather than a blank line
  • Andrew builds the pricing calculator: tier, locations, discounts, resulting margin, floor check
  • Split fee from media on every new media agreement using the campaign equation
Days 31–60
Audit the book of business
  • Score every active account against its tier floor
  • Sort into hold, re-scope, or re-price
  • Where an account is below floor, decide whether to lift the price or cut the promise — both are valid
Days 61–90
Renewal conversations
  • Staggered, not simultaneous — protect the 99% retention target
  • Lead with scope changes rather than price rises where the client is sensitive
  • Add a 4% annual escalator to every renewed agreement
06

What this model still needs

Three inputs would materially sharpen these numbers. None of them blocks the five decisions.

  • Current wage data. The $5,500 monthly figure and the 8-account caseload come from our team-margin sheet, which was last updated in 2023. Every floor in this document moves if that number has moved. This is the single input most worth refreshing.
  • Actual hours per account. The delivery caps above are budgets, not measurements — I found no time tracking against clients. Until we know what a signal session really costs us end to end, the caps are our best estimate rather than an observed figure.
  • A complete revenue list. This analysis is built from the eight agreements I could locate. A full MRR list by client would let us size how much revenue currently sits below floor, which is the number that determines how aggressive the day 31–90 phase should be.
